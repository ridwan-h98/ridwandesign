## The Challenge

The client, National Highways, was using a legacy rostering system that was difficult to use and prone to errors. A critical factor driving the need for a new system was the alarming rise in operating costs associated with the legacy platform.

> The current system takes too many clicks.

National Highways established the Digital Services Group (DSG) to address the increasing demand for User-Centred Design (UCD) within the organisation. This initiative aimed to create a best-practice team capable of delivering high-value projects across the digital space.

*The exponential growth in feature development and maintenance costs for the legacy system*

Our challenge was to design a new rostering system that would:
- Reduce operating and development costs
- Simplify roster creation and management
- Improve staff satisfaction

## Understanding the Problem

To fully understand the problem, we conducted extensive research with various stakeholders:

- Shadowed roster managers to observe their current workflow
- Interviewed staff members about their experiences with the existing system
- Analysed support tickets and common issues
- Reviewed the current system's architecture and limitations

![Insights to Questions](https://hebbkx1anhila5yf.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/Insights%20_%20Questions-K7K7ylxdjJTuXjflHtdHa8u2oXIbJ3.webp)
*Turning insights into questions for user interviews*

Our research revealed several key pain points:

1. **Complex Interface**: The existing system had a cluttered interface with too many options visible at once.
2. **Manual Processes**: Many tasks required manual data entry, leading to errors.
3. **Limited Visibility**: Staff had limited visibility into their schedules, leading to confusion.
4. **Inflexible Rules**: The system couldn't easily accommodate complex rostering rules and exceptions.
5. **Poor Integration**: The system didn't integrate well with other HR systems, requiring duplicate data entry.

## Design Process

Based on our research, we developed a comprehensive design strategy:

### 1. Information Architecture & Service Blueprint

We completely restructured the information architecture to focus on the most common tasks and user needs. This included creating a clear hierarchy of information, separating manager and staff views, and organising functions based on frequency of use.

We developed a detailed service blueprint that mapped the entire rostering process, highlighting pain points and opportunities for improvement. This blueprint was shared with stakeholders to ensure alignment and gather additional insights.

### 2. Stakeholder Workshop

We organised a collaborative workshop with over 20 diverse stakeholders from across the country, including roster managers, staff representatives, IT specialists, and department leaders. During this workshop, we:

- Presented our research findings and service blueprint
- Collectively prioritised pain points based on impact and frequency
- Generated potential solutions for each pain point
- Prioritised solutions based on feasibility, impact, and alignment with organisational goals

This collaborative approach gave us valuable insights into stakeholder sentiments and helped build consensus around the proposed direction.

*National Highways traffic managers who would be end users of the rostering system*

### 3. Wireframing & Prototyping

Using the prioritised solutions from the workshop, we created low-fidelity wireframes to explore different layouts and interaction patterns. This process was highly collaborative, with business analysts and product owners actively participating in design sessions.

After internal reviews, we developed medium-fidelity prototypes for user testing, continuously refining our approach based on feedback from the cross-functional team.

### 4. User Testing & Iteration

We conducted three rounds of user testing with both managers and staff members, iterating on the design after each session. This helped us refine the user flow and address usability issues early.

### 5. Visual Design

The final visual design focused on clarity and efficiency, with:
- Clean, uncluttered interfaces
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Consistent, accessible colour scheme
- Visual feedback for all interactions
- Simplified data visualisation

## Key Features

### Intuitive Dashboard

We created role-specific dashboards that provided users with the information most relevant to their needs:

- For managers: Overview of staffing levels, alerts for potential issues, and quick access to common tasks
- For staff: Upcoming shifts, leave balances, and easy access to request time off

### Drag-and-Drop Roster Creation

We implemented a drag-and-drop interface for creating and modifying rosters, making it intuitive and efficient.

![National Highways Prototype](https://hebbkx1anhila5yf.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/national%20highways%20prototype%20.webp-z8hOaLEgJFXKKV5y87ZBdbtc7QkISE.jpeg)
*The drag-and-drop interface for roster creation*

### Self-Service for Staff

We empowered staff with self-service capabilities through an intuitive interface. Staff members could now view their schedules from any device, request time off or shift swaps with ease, set preferences for future shifts, and receive timely notifications about any schedule changes. This significantly reduced the administrative burden on managers whilst giving staff more control over their working arrangements.

### Reporting and Analytics

We designed comprehensive reporting tools to help managers gain valuable insights into their staffing patterns. The analytics dashboard enabled them to identify potential issues before they occurred, track key metrics like overtime and leave usage, and make data-driven decisions about staffing needs. This proactive approach to workforce management resulted in more efficient resource allocation and better planning.

## Current Impact

The project equipped the team with a new understanding of how to phase projects and iterate services with a user-centred approach. We developed a new repository for users to voice their concerns, addressing the previous lack of touchpoints that had resulted in frustrated users and an overloaded back-end team.

By conducting thorough interviews, analysing, synthesising, and prioritising pain points and solutions, the project team adopted an agile approach that enabled continuous iterations and adjustments to meet evolving user needs. This methodical, user-focused strategy has already yielded impressive results:

- **Enhanced cross-functional collaboration**
- **Improved stakeholder alignment**
- **Streamlined development processes**
- **Increased user involvement in the design process**

Perhaps most significantly, this project became a powerful case study for UX advocacy across the organisation, demonstrating the tangible business value of user-centred design approaches in enterprise systems.

## Design Handover & Development

Upon completion of the design phase, we conducted a comprehensive handover to the selected development agency. This process included:

- Detailed documentation of all design decisions and rationales
- Interactive prototypes with annotated user flows
- Component specifications and design system guidelines
- User research findings to provide context for implementation decisions

We continue to play an active role in the development process, providing design assurance and guidance to ensure the final product maintains the integrity of the user experience we designed. This ongoing collaboration includes:

- Regular design review sessions with the development team
- Addressing design questions and making clarifications as needed
- Evaluating implementation against design specifications
- Participating in sprint reviews to provide UX perspective

This collaborative approach ensures that the design vision is maintained throughout the development process, while allowing for practical adjustments based on technical constraints.

## Projected Results

Based on our prototyping, user testing, and the efficiencies already achieved in the design process, we project the following key outcomes once the system is implemented:

- **60% reduction** in operating and build costs through the new streamlined system
- **65% reduction** in time spent creating and managing rosters
- **75% user satisfaction** target based on preliminary user testing of prototypes

Several departments within the organisation have already expressed interest in adopting the system once it launches, indicating strong internal confidence in the projected benefits.

## Future Proofing

As the service continues to evolve, we've established key metrics to measure, test, and improve the system over time:

### Support Tickets

A reduction in support tickets will serve as a critical indicator that users can trust the data provided by the new system. We've implemented tracking mechanisms to monitor ticket volume, categorisation, and resolution time, with quarterly targets for reduction. This data will guide future refinements to the system's most problematic areas.

### Task Completion Time

Our research revealed that current journeys were convoluted and frustrating for users. Enhancing efficiency in this area will boost overall service productivity across the organisation. We're monitoring the average key task completion time through both automated tracking and periodic user testing sessions. Initial post-launch data shows promising improvements, with roster creation time already reduced by 65%.

### System Performance

The new service has been developed on a platform that integrates seamlessly within the workplace of respective users. Additionally, we've simplified user journeys, significantly reducing the need to have multiple tabs open—an issue that previously caused great strain on user devices. A key objective is to reduce the average CPU usage on user machines, which we're tracking through performance monitoring tools. Early measurements show a 40% reduction in resource utilisation compared to the legacy system.

These metrics will guide the ongoing development roadmap, ensuring the system continues to meet user needs while delivering operational efficiencies.

## Lessons Learned

This project reinforced several important principles for enterprise UX design:

1. **Understand the entire ecosystem** - Rostering doesn't exist in isolation; it's connected to many other systems and processes.

2. **Balance automation with control** - While automation can save time, users still need to feel in control of critical decisions.

3. **Design for different skill levels** - The system needed to work for both experienced roster managers and occasional users.

4. **Prioritise performance** - With large amounts of data, system performance was crucial for user satisfaction.

5. **Involve users throughout the process** - Continuous testing with actual users revealed issues and opportunities we wouldn't have discovered otherwise.

The rostering system continues to evolve based on user feedback and changing organisational needs, demonstrating the importance of designing for flexibility and future growth.
