## Challenge

National Highways had established their Digital Services Group (DSG) to address the growing demand for user-centered design across the organisation. However, the newly formed team faced significant challenges in establishing consistent methodologies, building internal capabilities, and demonstrating value to the wider organisation.

The team operated with varying levels of UX maturity—some members had extensive experience while others were transitioning from adjacent roles. This disparity created inconsistencies in output quality and approach. Additionally, the team struggled to effectively collaborate with technical teams and business stakeholders, often being brought into projects too late in the process to make meaningful impact.

The team structure consisted of:

**Capgemini Invent Consultants:**
- 3 Service Designers
- 1 Automation Consultant
- 1 Agile Coach

**National Highways DSG Team:**
- 10 team members with varying levels of UX experience

This balanced team composition allowed us to provide specialized expertise while working closely with the internal team members to build sustainable capabilities.

> We needed to transform a group of individuals into a cohesive team that could deliver consistent UX work.

To address these challenges, we needed to develop a comprehensive understanding of the team's current capabilities. We implemented periodic maturity assessments using a customised framework based on Nielsen Norman Group's UX maturity model, adapted specifically for government digital services. These assessments evaluated capabilities across six dimensions:

1. User Research & Insights
2. Design Process & Methodology
3. Collaboration & Communication
4. Tools & Technology
5. Governance & Standards
6. Impact Measurement

The initial assessment revealed significant gaps in standardised processes, research methodologies, and strategic influence within the organisation. This baseline gave us clear direction on where to focus our improvement efforts and allowed us to create a targeted strategy for upskilling the DSG team.

## Working with the Internal Team

I worked as part of a team of five Capgemini Invent consultants embedded within the DSG team for an 11-month engagement. Rather than taking a theoretical approach, we adopted a "learn by doing" methodology, working alongside team members on actual projects while simultaneously building their capabilities.

Our approach was deliberately hands-on and collaborative. Each consultant was paired with specific DSG team members based on complementary skills and development needs. These partnerships created a safe space for knowledge transfer, experimentation, and growth.

The embedded model allowed us to:

- Observe real-time challenges and provide immediate guidance
- Demonstrate best practices in context rather than in abstract
- Build trust through shared project accountability
- Identify systemic issues that wouldn't be apparent in workshop settings
- Adapt our approach based on individual learning styles and needs

We established weekly reflection sessions where team members could discuss challenges, share learnings, and celebrate wins. These sessions created a culture of continuous improvement and psychological safety that was crucial for the team's development.

## Operating within the Organisation

Understanding the organisational context was critical to our success. National Highways operates in a complex environment with multiple stakeholders, strict regulatory requirements, and established ways of working that weren't always aligned with user-centered design principles.

We engaged with project stakeholders to understand the needs of the wider business. This helped us identify where to upskill the team to deliver the highest value. Through these engagements, we gained valuable insights into stakeholder perceptions of the DSG team and identified opportunities to demonstrate value.

We discovered that many stakeholders had limited understanding of UX and its potential impact, which informed our approach to communication and education. By focusing on the specific needs of project stakeholders, we were able to tailor our capability building efforts to address the most pressing business challenges.

## Understanding the Team

To develop a comprehensive understanding of the DSG team's capabilities, we implemented a multi-faceted approach combining formal assessments with day-to-day observations.

We established structured feedback loops through:
- Weekly one-on-one coaching sessions with each team member
- Bi-weekly team retrospectives focused on process improvements
- Monthly capability reviews with team leads
- Quarterly formal assessments against our maturity framework

These mechanisms allowed us to track progress at both individual and team levels, identifying patterns and adjusting our approach accordingly.

The initial assessment revealed several key insights:

1. **Varied Skill Levels**: The team had pockets of excellence in visual design and prototyping but lacked depth in user research and strategic UX thinking.

2. **Process Inconsistencies**: Each team member had developed their own approach to projects, leading to inconsistent outputs and difficulty in collaboration.

3. **Tool Proficiency Gaps**: While the team had access to industry-standard tools, many members weren't utilizing their full capabilities.

4. **Documentation Challenges**: Knowledge sharing was hindered by inconsistent documentation practices and lack of centralised resources.

5. **Strategic Influence**: The team struggled to articulate the value of their work in terms that resonated with business stakeholders.

Based on these insights, we developed personalised development plans for each team member, aligning individual growth with the team's overall maturity goals.

> Observations revealed not just skill gaps, but also mindset and confidence issues.

We implemented a skills matrix that visualised the team's collective capabilities, highlighting areas of strength and opportunities for knowledge sharing. This transparency fostered a culture of peer learning and reduced dependency on external consultants over time.

## Shaping Processes and Ways of Working

With a clear understanding of both the organisational context and team capabilities, we focused on establishing robust processes and ways of working that would outlast our engagement.

### Project Delivery Framework

We developed a flexible UX project framework that could scale from small enhancements to major initiatives. This framework included:

- Project kickoff templates with stakeholder alignment activities
- Research planning guides and participant recruitment processes
- Design review protocols with clear acceptance criteria
- Handover procedures for development teams
- Post-implementation evaluation methods

The framework was deliberately lightweight, focusing on principles and outcomes rather than rigid procedures. This approach allowed the team to adapt methods to project-specific needs while maintaining consistency in overall approach.

### Knowledge Building Workshops

We designed and delivered six comprehensive workshops addressing key capability gaps:

1. **Strategic UX Research**: Moving beyond tactical usability testing to research that informs product strategy and roadmaps.

2. **Design Systems Thinking**: Establishing consistent patterns and components to improve efficiency and user experience coherence.

3. **Stakeholder Management**: Techniques for building relationships, managing expectations, and communicating UX value effectively.

4. **Service Design Methods**: Expanding the team's toolkit beyond UI design to holistic service thinking.

5. **Measuring UX Impact**: Approaches for establishing baselines and demonstrating the business value of UX improvements.

6. **Collaborative Design Techniques**: Methods for inclusive design sessions that leverage diverse perspectives.

Each workshop combined theoretical foundations with practical exercises based on actual National Highways projects, ensuring immediate applicability of new knowledge.

### Agile Integration

Working closely with the Agile Coach and Automation Consultant, we developed approaches for integrating UX activities into the existing agile delivery framework. Key innovations included:

- "Research Spikes" that allowed for user insights ahead of development sprints
- Design critique sessions integrated into sprint ceremonies
- UX debt tracking alongside technical debt
- Collaborative story mapping techniques that brought designers and developers together

These integrations helped bridge the traditional gap between design and development, creating a more cohesive delivery process.

### Documentation and Knowledge Management

To address challenges in knowledge sharing, we established:

- Standardised research repositories with consistent tagging and retrieval methods
- Project wikis with templated sections for key UX artifacts
- A UX playbook documenting common scenarios and recommended approaches

These resources created a foundation for consistent practice and efficient onboarding of new team members.

## Results

Over the 11-month engagement, the DSG team underwent a transformation, evolving from a collection of individuals working in silos to a cohesive team with shared methodologies and clear purpose.

Key improvements included:

- **Enhanced UX Maturity**: The team developed more consistent approaches to user research and design
- **Improved Cross-functional Collaboration**: Better relationships with development teams and business units
- **Knowledge Sharing**: Active community of practice with regular show-and-tell sessions
- **Team Confidence**: Greater willingness to challenge assumptions and advocate for user needs

The most significant indicator of success was the team's ability to independently apply the methodologies and approaches we had introduced. By the end of our engagement, the DSG team was self-sufficient in running workshops, conducting research, and managing stakeholder relationships—skills that had previously been gaps in their capability.

## Conclusion

The transformation of National Highways' Digital Services Group demonstrates the power of structured capability building within an existing team. Rather than relying on external resources indefinitely, investing in internal capability development created sustainable change that continues to deliver value.

Several key factors contributed to the success of this initiative:

1. **Embedded Approach**: Working alongside team members on real projects created contextual learning opportunities that theoretical training alone couldn't provide.

2. **Holistic Focus**: Addressing team dynamics, processes, and organisational context alongside individual skills development created comprehensive change.

3. **Measurement Framework**: The maturity assessment provided clear direction and allowed for objective tracking of progress.

4. **Tailored Development**: Recognising and accommodating different learning styles and starting points within the team.

5. **Stakeholder Engagement**: Actively involving business leaders in the transformation journey created organisational buy-in.

The DSG team now serves as a center of excellence within National Highways, championing user-centered design principles across the organisation and mentoring other teams beginning their UX journey. Their transformation has become a case study within the wider government digital community, demonstrating how internal teams can be elevated to deliver world-class user experiences.

This approach to team building—focusing on capability development rather than just project delivery—offers a blueprint for organisations looking to build sustainable internal UX competency in complex environments.
